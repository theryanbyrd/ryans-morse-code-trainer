Morse code FAQ

The questions people actually ask when they start learning CW.

Short version. Learn each letter at full speed from the start, practise a few minutes a day, and listen more than you send. Dit Dah Me is free, works offline, and needs no account.

How long does it take to learn Morse code?

Most people can recognise all 26 letters within two to four weeks of short daily practice. Getting to a conversational speed of around 20 words per minute usually takes a few months. Short sessions every day beat long sessions once a week, because recognition is a reflex you build, not a fact you memorise.

What speed should I start at?

Start at the speed you want to end up at, and slow down the gaps rather than the characters. That is the core of the Koch method: each letter is sent at full speed from day one, with extra space between letters while you are learning. If you learn letters slowly you end up counting dots, which is a habit you then have to unlearn.

What is the Koch method?

A training method that starts you with just two characters sent at full target speed, and adds one new character each time you can copy the current set at 90% accuracy. It avoids the plateau that comes from learning slowly and speeding up later. Dit Dah Me includes the full 39-lesson Koch course.

Do I need an amateur radio licence to learn Morse code?

No. Morse code is free to learn and practise offline with no licence at all. You only need a licence to transmit on amateur radio bands. In the United States that is the FCC Technician licence, which no longer requires a Morse exam.

Is Morse code still used today?

Yes. Amateur radio operators use CW daily because it carries further on less power than voice, and it is still used in aviation for navigation beacon identifiers. It is also a genuinely useful accessible input method for people who can operate one switch.

What do dit and dah mean?

Dit is the short signal and dah is the long one, and they are named after how they sound rather than how they look. A dah is three times the length of a dit. Saying the rhythm out loud as dit and dah is far easier to internalise than reading dots and dashes on a page.

Can I learn Morse code with a screen reader or a single switch?

Yes. Dit Dah Me supports VoiceOver throughout, one-switch scanning, and a single-key mode where a short press is a dit and a long press is a dah. It was built from Ace Centre's Morse Learn, which exists specifically for switch users.

What is a straight key?

The classic single-lever Morse key, where you control the length of every signal by hand. Dit Dah Me has a straight-key mode where a tap is a dit and a hold is a dah, which is the closest thing to a real key you can practise on a phone.

How do I practise copying real Morse?

Listening practice matters more than sending. Use the Hear letters and Hear words drills, then the 20 scripted on-air conversations, which use the shorthand real operators actually send: CQ, 73, QTH and so on.
